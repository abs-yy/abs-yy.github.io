Welcome to my home page! I'm Yuki Yoshida, a researcher at the [Institute of Agrobiological Sciences, NARO](https://www.naro.go.jp/laboratory/nias/index.html) (National Agriculture and Food Research Organization), Japan. My research centres on **anhydrobiosis** — the remarkable ability of certain organisms to survive near-complete desiccation and revive upon rehydration. I combine genomics, cell biology, and bioinformatics to understand how this capacity evolved and how it can be harnessed.

## Background
I received my Ph.D. from Keio University, Graduate School of Media and Governance, under Prof. Masaru Tomita and Prof. Kazuharu Arakawa, where I worked on comparative genomics of anhydrobiotic tardigrades and midges. During my doctoral studies I also worked with Prof. Takahiro Kikawada at NARO, and I subsequently held a postdoctoral position at The University of Tokyo under Prof. Yuichi Wakamoto. I joined NARO as a staff researcher in 2023.

## Research topics

### Anhydrobiosis — a latent state of life
Some organisms can lose nearly all of their cellular water and enter a state of suspended animation, then resume life upon rehydration. I study the genomic and molecular basis of this phenomenon in the sleeping chironomid *Polypedilum vanderplanki* and in tardigrades, asking how desiccation tolerance arose and what molecular safeguards keep cells intact in the dry state.

### Pv11 cell line — a model system for anhydrobiosis
The Pv11 cell line, derived from *P. vanderplanki* embryos, is the only known animal cell line capable of surviving complete desiccation. We are developing Pv11 as a tractable genetic model: we recently established a **recombinase-mediated cassette exchange (RMCE)** system that allows precise and efficient gene integration, opening the door to systematic functional genomics in this unique cell line.

Related papers:
1. ["Flp/FRT" site-specific recombination system in the anhydrobiotic Pv11 cells](https://abs-yy.github.io/publication/2024-11-15-CryobiolCryotechnol-RMCE)
2. [A sodium-dependent trehalose transporter contributes to anhydrobiosis in insect cell line, Pv11](https://abs-yy.github.io/publication/2024-03-29-PNAS)

### Genomics of non-model organisms
Many of the most fascinating biological traits — extreme stress tolerance, unusual life histories, novel body plans — occur only in species that have historically been overlooked by genomics. I have participated in genome and transcriptome projects spanning tardigrades, chironomids, spiders, and barnacles, contributing to the broader picture of how genomes encode extraordinary phenotypes.
